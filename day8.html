Past Due Account Workflow

What Is a Past Due Account?

A partner whose subscription payment has failed or is overdue. The goal is a clear, professional heads-up and a chance to update their payment before the account is deactivated. One attempt per day, consistent documentation, and a clean cancellation if they don't respond.

Past Due Call Process — Step by Step

1
Check the time zone before callingLook up the partner's area code on Google to confirm their time zone. No calls after 6 PM their local time.
2
Block the call on your calendarAdd the partner name and scheduled call time as a calendar event so you have structure and can track attempts.
3
Call once. Leave a voicemail if no answer."Hi, this is Rick from the partner support team at Batch. I'm reaching out about your account — there's an outstanding invoice I'd love to help you sort out. Please give us a call back or reply to our email. Thank you."
4
Send a follow-up emailKeep it short and professional. Include the invoice amount, date, and a clear next step (update payment or contact you to discuss options).
5
Document the attempt in Airtable commentsNote the date, what was done, and current status. If the partner responds via email and promises to pay — log it, no further calls needed.
6
Second attempt next day if no responseTry once more the following day. If still unresponsive — proceed to cancellation with a notification email.
7
Assess signal before attempting a saveIf the partner responds and wants to cancel — run a churn call. Apply signal rules: strong/medium within 3 months = save attempt. Weak signal or 30+ days overdue = cancel gracefully.

Boost & Party Pro — Handling Protocol

These Are Not Your Partners to Own

Boost and Party Pro partners are handled by their dedicated account managers — Aislin and Rachel. Your role is to flag, inform, and route — not to take ownership of their cases. The PX team does not independently handle Boost/Party Pro disputes, cancellations, or retention calls without first checking with Aislin or Rachel.

What Makes Boost & Party Pro Different

  • They have access to features core partners don't: email campaigns and SMS campaigns
  • Do not offer email or SMS marketing as a retention lever to core partners — those features are not available at that tier
  • If you're not trained on what products were promised to a Boost/Party Pro partner, you can't accurately assess whether service was delivered — which means you can't run a retention call or dispute properly without AM involvement

2
Message Aislin (and Rachel if needed) in #batch-partner-support on SlackDo NOT tag them in Airtable — they prefer Slack. Include: partner name, Intercom conversation link, vendor directory link, dispute or issue link, and a brief summary.
3

4
CC Stefan on the conversationAlways CC Stefan when looping in Aislin/Rachel. This protects you if there's any later disagreement about who was responsible for what.
5
Wait for their direction before actingTheir go-signal for one task (e.g. accept the dispute) does NOT mean go-signal for all tasks (e.g. also do the cancellation). Get explicit approval for each action.

SEO ROI Conversations & Call Debrief

SEO Is Compounding — Partners Need to Hear This Early

Two of today's scheduled calls came from partners with ROI concerns after only 2 months. This is the most common objection you'll face. The answer: SEO results build slowly in the first 30 days, accelerate through month 2, and by month 3 the audience and data are in place for real traction. Manage this expectation on every onboarding and follow-up call — before partners get frustrated enough to cancel.

How to Frame the SEO Compounding Argument

  • After 65 days, one partner had 1,000 search impressions — roughly 33 times per day their listing appeared in search results
  • Frame it: "Day 1, you might show up 5 times a day. By week 2, it's 20. By month 1, it's 30–35. Right now you're building that curve. Month 3 is when it really bends."
  • Show the data: impressions, favorites added, itinerary ads. Even 2 itinerary adds and 3 favorites in 65 days is real traction.
  • If they have favorites added: tell them that real potential planners have bookmarked their listing — bookings typically follow within 30–90 days
  • Never promise a booking date. Frame everything as compounding probability, not guaranteed outcomes.
  • Use the city demand data to anchor expectations: show how many parties are actually being created in their specific city, not the platform-wide 3 million figure

City Demand Data — Use It Every Time

  • The city demand data feature shows how many parties are being created in a specific city — this is the realistic customer pool
  • Actual demand per city is 200–2,000 parties. Use the city number on partner calls, not the platform-wide total.
  • If a partner's listing isn't tailored to bachelorette groups (90% of Batch audience), that's actionable: help them reposition their description and photos toward that audience
  • City demand data access is in the partner analytics dashboard — make sure you know how to pull it before calls
