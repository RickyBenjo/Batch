View Naming Convention

  • Prefix all your views with NW so they're easy to find and clearly owned by the Neoworks team

Stripe Subscription Phasing

🎉
Automated Price Reversion — Unlocked

Rick and Cliff figured out how to use Stripe's phased subscription feature to automatically revert discounted pricing back to the original price after 3 months — no more manual Google Sheet tracking, no more calendar reminders to go back and fix the price. Stefan and Luis approved moving forward with this method. Share this win with the Batch Leadership Slack channel.

Why This Matters

When you save a partner by offering $99/month (or another discounted price), that discount is supposed to last only 3 months before reverting to the standard rate. Previously, this was tracked manually in a Google Sheet and required someone to go back into Stripe and manually update the price. With phased subscriptions, Stripe handles the reversion automatically.

How Stripe Subscription Phases Work

  • A subscription can be divided into phases — each phase has its own price, duration, and end date
  • When one phase ends, Stripe automatically moves to the next phase and applies the new pricing
  • This requires using the classic editor in Stripe — the current default editor does not support phased subscriptions
  • You access the classic editor by clicking "Switch to classic version of the subscription editor" when updating a subscription
  • Once a subscription has phases, any future updates must also be done through phases (not the standard "update subscription" button)

Step-by-Step: Setting Up a 3-Month Discounted Phase

1
Go to the partner's subscription in StripeFind the active subscription. Note the current price and the next billing date.
2
Click "Update Subscription" → switch to classic editorThe system warns you that scheduling is disabled if phases already exist — ignore if doing it fresh
3
Edit Phase 1: current billing to next billing dateKeep the original price for this phase — this covers the current paid period without creating prorated charges
4
Click "Add Phase" → set Phase 2: discounted pricingSet the price to $99/month (or agreed discount). Set duration to 3 months from the next billing date.
5
Click "Add Phase" again → set Phase 3: original pricing, no end dateRestore the original price (e.g. $150/month). Leave end date empty — this phase runs forever until changed.
6
Review the summary on the right side panelConfirm it shows: current billing date → next billing at original price → discount starts → discount ends → original price resumes. Must match exactly.
7
Click "Update Subscription" to saveThe system will show "end of current billing period" as the apply date. Confirm and save.
8
Block a calendar reminder for 1 day after Phase 2 startsCheck the partner's invoice history to confirm no double-billing occurred. Stripe has occasionally double-charged when subscription phases are changed mid-cycle.
⚠️ Do NOT Change the Product — Only the Price

When setting up phased pricing, only change the price amount on the existing product — do not remove the product and add a new one. Adding/removing products creates a different line item in Stripe invoices and can trigger proration charges. Keep the same product, just update the price per phase.

Past Due Partner Calls

What Are Past Due Calls?

Past due calls are outbound calls to partners whose subscription payment has failed or is overdue. The goal is simple: let them know their account is at risk, find out if they want to stay, and give them the chance to update their payment method. If they're upset, unresponsive, or clearly done — proceed to cancellation. Do not force it.

Past Due Call Script

1
Opening"Hi [name], this is Rick from the partner support team at Batch. I'm reaching out because I noticed your account has an outstanding invoice. Is this a good time to chat for a couple of minutes?"
2
Explain the situation"I see that your most recent invoice from [date] is currently unpaid. Your listings are still live for now, but if the payment isn't settled, the account and listings will be deactivated."
3
Check intent"I just wanted to make sure this was on your radar — do you want to continue with your Batch partnership, or are you thinking of making a change?"
4
If they want to stayGuide them to update their payment method. Confirm timing. Send a follow-up email with the link if needed. Note in Airtable.
5
If they want to cancel or push backTreat it as a churn call. Use your retention levers if signal is medium or higher. If weak signal or they're firm — proceed to cancellation. End of current billing period, not immediate.

Batch Billing Model — Prepaid

Batch uses a prepaid model — partners pay before the service period begins, not after. A payment on April 28 covers April 28 to May 28. This means if a partner is past due on an April invoice, they have technically not yet been served for that period. Use this context when deciding whether to void, pause, or proceed to cancellation — and when explaining the situation to partners who are confused about what they're being charged for.

Partner Calendar Types

Why Calendars Exist

Some partners take bookings directly through Batch using the platform's calendar system. Their availability windows need to be set up correctly so customers only see times when the partner is actually available. Wrong calendars = customers booking at times the partner can't fulfill = angry customers, refund requests, and Trustpilot complaints. Calendar setup was Cliff's first assignment at Batch, and Kring was trained extensively on it too — it's foundational.

The Two Calendar Types

TypeHow It WorksWhen to Use
Static CalendarPartner selects available days (Mon–Sun). All selected days show 9 AM–9 PM as open slots automatically.Partner is available standard hours every day they're open. Simple, no exceptions.
Dynamic CalendarPartner provides specific time slots per day — e.g., 7–8 AM and 2–6 PM on Fridays only, 9 AM–6 PM Mon–Thu. Each slot is set manually.Partner has irregular hours, blackout times within a day, or seasonal availability windows. More complex but more accurate.

How to Find & Edit a Partner's Calendar

1
Go to Admin → find the partner's experience listingClick the "Open in BAT" button at the top right of the listing — this takes you to the BAT admin portal
2
Scroll to the bottom of the listing in BATFind the "Full Availability Calendar" section — this shows all existing calendar entries
3
Click Edit on an existing entry or create a new oneFor static: select available days and save. For dynamic: add individual time slots per day.
4
Always follow the partner's stated availabilityNever add availability the partner didn't provide. If unsure, ask before setting up. Over-promising availability = customer complaints.
5
Log it in the Calendar tracking view in AirtableAdd date, your name, partner name, number of calendars worked on, experience link. The automated field tracking doesn't persist — manual log is your backup.

When to Cancel Without a Retention Attempt

  • Partner has zero itinerary adds after 90+ days — not worth the call
  • Partner is very upset and refuses any conversation — let them cancel gracefully, avoid the Trustpilot risk

Dispute Handling — Full SOP

🚨 Most Important Rule in This Entire SOP

Dispute deadlines do not pause for partner conversations. A missed deadline = automatic loss, no exceptions. Always submit your counter evidence before the deadline — regardless of whether you've spoken to the partner or not. The partner call is a retention tool. The dispute submission is a financial protection tool. Run them in parallel, never in sequence.

What Is a Subscription Dispute?

  • A dispute (chargeback) is when a partner contacts their bank or card issuer to reverse a Batch subscription charge
  • Stripe notifies you when a dispute is filed — you then have a window to submit counter-evidence
  • If you submit strong evidence and win, the charge is returned to Batch
  • If you miss the deadline or don't submit, Batch loses automatically — no exceptions, no appeals
  • Applies to monthly, annual, and any digital product billing (marketplace listing, analytics access)

Urgency Levels by Time Remaining

Time RemainingUrgencyRequired Action
Less than 7 daysCRITICALInvestigate and submit same day. Call partner only after submission.
7–14 daysHIGHInvestigate and submit within 24 hours. Partner call same day if possible.
15–30 daysMODERATEPrepare counter immediately. Attempt partner call. Submit by day 3 if no withdrawal.
30–75 daysSTANDARDFull investigation and partner outreach. Submit no later than day 5. Set a calendar reminder — do not let it fall off your radar.

Step-by-Step Dispute Process

1
Receive & Triage (Day 0)Log in the dispute tracker immediately. Note the deadline. Flag anything under 14 days as urgent — escalate to supervisor if needed. Identify the dispute reason (service quality, unrecognized charge, cancellation, etc.).
2
Investigate & Gather Evidence (30 min or less for an expert)Pull from admin/platform records: billing history, invoice history, onboarding completion date, signed terms URL, listing publish date and active status, confirmed bookings and revenue generated, cancellation request date (if applicable), subscription billing date vs. cancellation request date. Verify the 14-day cancellation notice window if relevant.
3
Generate & Review Response Letter (10 min)Run evidence through the Response Letter Tool. Review all dates, amounts, and policy references for accuracy. Add any sections the tool missed (e.g. missed cancellation window). Complex or high-value disputes should have a second set of eyes.
4
Decide: Counter or AcceptCounter if: service was delivered, terms were signed, evidence is clean, and charges are legitimate. Accept if: there's a clear billing error, the charge was unauthorized, or evidence doesn't support Batch's position. Do not counter a dispute you intend to accept — it complicates resolution.
5
Submit to Stripe (5–10 min)Upload all evidence documents. Attach the finalized response letter. Submit before the deadline. Log the submission timestamp in the dispute tracker.
6
Partner Call — Request WithdrawalCall the partner to explain and ask them to withdraw the dispute. If they agree: wait max 3 business days for Stripe to reflect it (only attempt this if you have 15+ days remaining). If they decline or are unreachable: your submitted counter is already in place — no further action needed. Log the call attempt and outcome in CRM.
7
Monitor & Follow UpCheck Stripe dashboard periodically — do not rely on email notifications alone. Do not mark the dispute resolved until Stripe confirms "Won."
8
Log & CloseUpdate the dispute tracker with the final outcome (Won / Lost / Withdrawn). Log all CRM notes. If lost — flag for supervisor review and identify any process improvement.
