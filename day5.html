After-Call Workflow

Core Rule: Record First, Execute Second

Right after every call, submit the Airtable cross-team request form before doing anything else. This keeps a paper trail of what was promised, who's responsible, and what's been completed.

Step-by-Step: After Every Churn Call

1
Go to Airtable → Cross-Team Request FormSelect Partner Experience Team for all partner support tasks. Select Member Experience Team only for customer/booking issues.
2
Find the Vendor IDGo to the partner's profile page in the Vendor Directory. The ID is in the URL. Copy it directly — don't retype.
3
Fill in Request DetailsList each task separately: "Listing optimization — all experiences," "Ratings and reviews upload," "Blog post creation," "UGC tool activation," "Media shuffle." Specify which listing(s) if partner has more than one.
4
Set Priority LevelOn-fire = today. High priority = 24–48 hours (use this for after-call tasks — matches what you told the partner). Low priority = normal queue.
5
Submit + Notify in SlackCreate the record first, then notify teammates with the Airtable link. Never rely on Slack alone.
6
Assign DirectlyAssign the task to a specific teammate in Airtable. Don't let teammates self-assign — it leads to avoidance and uneven distribution.
7
For Cancellations: Send Intercom Message ImmediatelyUse the Intercom macro (backslash → "cancel" → select PX Cancellation at End of Subscription Term). Edit the billing end date. Send within 2–3 minutes of ending the call.
8
Prepare for Next CallAirtable request: 1–3 min. Intercom cancellation: 2–3 min. Then straight into pre-call prep for the next partner.

Waiting for Partner vs In Progress

If you've done your part but the partner hasn't uploaded photos or added reviews yet, mark the field as Waiting for Partner. This lets you follow up proactively during slow periods.

After-Call Task Execution Checklist

  • Re-optimize all listings — run CoStar on each, update title, description, tags
  • Copy ratings — use "Add to Listing" button to transfer up to 80 reviews from one listing to another
  • Send blog post email macro — ask for blog assets (photos, story, key details)
  • Send UGC Collection Tool intro email — use macro with screenshots and tool link
  • Shuffle media — rearrange photos and videos; alternate video and photo; best photo first
  • Notify partner via email — one consolidated email OR individual emails per offer

Retention Tiers — Updated System

What Changed

The 1-month pause offer has been eliminated. It created extra work and partners who took it usually churned anyway. Replaced with a structured 3-tier system.

Tier 1 — Free Modules (Always First)

Activate via the BPM Module checkbox in the partner's Airtable record.

Signal StrengthModules to Offer
Strong Signal (5+ itinerary adds)All 3: Content Creator Network + UGC Collection + City Demand Data
Medium Signal (1–4 itinerary adds)All 3: Content Creator Network + UGC Collection + City Demand Data
Weak Signal (0 itinerary adds)City Demand Data only — shows them where the market is heading

Tier 2 — Quarterly-to-Monthly Billing Switch

  • When to offer: Partner cites cash flow concerns, or they are outside their commitment period
  • When NOT to offer: If a partner is still within a commitment period and pays quarterly — switching lets them cancel after one month and dispute the rest
  • How: Change billing cycle in Stripe — don't delete and recreate the subscription

Tier 3 — Temporary Price Reduction (Max 3 Months)

  • Starting offer: 25% discount (e.g., $150/mo → $112.50)
  • Price floor: Never discount a partner already at $99/mo
  • Maximum duration: 3 months, tracked in the Pricing Reversion Task tab
  • Ideal timing: At the 7-day-before-billing-end meeting, after showing improved metrics
  • If they push for more: Escalate to $150/mo flat, then $99/mo — in that order
  • Seasonal partners: If they cancel anyway, advise them to return 3 months before peak season
Offer Timing Matters

Don't offer discounts immediately when a partner requests cancellation — it signals desperation. The first 6 months should be used to prove value. Only offer pricing concessions when you have data showing improvement, or when all other levers have been refused.

Stripe Billing & Price Reversion

Why the Old Process Was Broken

The previous workflow deleted the old Stripe product and created a new one at the discounted price. When it was time to revert, there was no record of the original price. The new process edits in-place and uses a dedicated tracking tab.

New Process: Temporary Price Cut in Stripe

1
Find the Partner's Subscription in StripeOpen Stripe → search partner name or email → open their subscription.
2
Edit the Existing Price (Do NOT Delete)Click on the current product price → Edit → change the amount directly. Never delete the product and add a new one.
3
Schedule the ChangeSet it to the end of the current billing cycle so the discount starts on their next bill, not mid-cycle.
4
Log in the Pricing Reversion Task TabRecord: partner name, original price, discounted price, start date, and reversion date (3 months out).
5
Email Partner ~1 Week Before Reversion"Your promotional rate ends on [date] and your subscription will return to $[original] on [date]. Here's what we've accomplished together in the last 3 months…"
6
On Reversion Date: Edit Price BackRepeat step 2 in reverse. Check the Pricing Reversion Task tab weekly so nothing is missed.
⚠️ Never Delete & Re-Add a Stripe Product

Deleting creates a new subscription record, loses billing history, and makes reversion harder. Always edit the existing price in place.

Cancellation in Stripe — Quick Recap

1
Find subscription → Click 3 dots → Cancel Subscription
2
Always choose "End of Current Period"Never cancel immediately unless partner demands it and you've confirmed with Stefan. They paid for the time — they keep it.
3

4
Add a note in StripeWhy they cancelled, what was offered, outcome. Useful for Stefan's analysis and future dispute prevention.

Dispute Management

Tool: Batch Dispute Evidence Builder

Use the Evidence Builder before deciding whether to counter or accept a dispute. It assesses win probability based on dispute type, time elapsed, services provided, and documentation available.

Process: When a Partner Files a Dispute

1
Run the Dispute Evidence BuilderAssess win probability. Identify the dispute type.
2
Call the Partner FirstBefore submitting counter evidence, call directly. Request they withdraw the dispute. Most disputes are really retention problems in disguise.
3a
If Partner WithdrawsConfirm via email immediately. Document the withdrawal. Proceed with retention offers.
3b
If Partner Refuses and Wants to CancelCancel their account but still counter the dispute citing Batch Partner Policies. Don't forfeit a dispute just because they're cancelling.
3c
If Partner Refuses but Doesn't CancelCounter using Evidence Builder output with all relevant documentation: listing activity, communications, billing records, services rendered.
4
Add a Detailed Note in StripeRegardless of outcome — accepted or countered, and why. Creates a paper trail for Stefan.

Case Study
Morena Miami Tan — Accepted Dispute

The Situation

Partner disputed a $200 charge as "fraudulent." Investigation: paid for 5 months (~$1,000 total) before their listing went live on April 29. The disputed charge was from April 11 — 18 days before their listing was even published.

Decision: Accept the Dispute

Batch failed to deliver the service during that 18-day window. The partner received no value from that specific charge — refund was justified even though they labelled it "fraudulent."

Lesson

The dispute label doesn't always match the underlying issue. Ask: did we actually provide the service? If no — accept. If yes — counter with evidence.

What "Win Probability" Actually Means

  • High win probability: Listing activity records, email logs, optimization history, partner was active
  • Low win probability: Partner never had their listing live, minimal communication history, or evidence of a service failure on Batch's side
  • Current win rate is ~6% — very low. Adding Stripe notes after every call is one key way to build better dispute evidence going forward

Batch Partner Policies

Where to Find Them

Admin Panel → Submit Service Request → click the "Batch Partner Policies" hyperlink. Review before any dispute or commitment enforcement conversation.

Key Policy Clauses

PolicyWhat It MeansHow to Use It
14-Day Cancellation NoticePartners must give 14 days notice before cancelling"Per our partner policy, we require 14 days notice — which gives me time to put together a proper optimization plan before your listing deactivates."
Commitment TermsPartners agreed to a minimum commitment period at sign-upReframe as SEO investment: "The commitment period is there because SEO takes 3 months to compound." Never weaponize it — use it as context.
No Refund PolicyDefault is no refunds on subscription paymentsThis is the baseline. Never promise refunds without Stefan approval.
Non-DisclosurePartners agreed not to disclose platform detailsRarely enforced — reference only if partner is publicly disparaging the platform.

Enforcing the 12-Month Commitment — When to Use It

  • Use it once: Enforce on the first call. Schedule cancellation for end of commitment term and fill that window with high-value optimizations.
  • Don't use it twice: If a partner already tried to cancel before and you enforced the policy then, do not enforce it again. Process the cancellation immediately — otherwise it looks predatory.
  • High frustration = immediate cancel: If a partner is extremely upset or claims they were misled, cancelling immediately is better than enforcing policy and risking a dispute.
  • Enforcement creates a window: A 6-month remaining commitment = 6 months to demonstrate value and convert them to bookable.
⚠️ Refunds Always Require Stefan Approval

Never promise a refund on a call without Stefan's sign-off. Say: "I'll submit that to our team for review and follow up within 24–48 hours." Don't say "I'll see what I can do" — it implies a yes that may not come through.

Live Call Case Studies

Context: Live Shadowing — May 1

Three real churn calls handled during live shadowing. Each illustrates a different scenario and decision framework. Study the reasoning behind each outcome.

Churned
Blumonsense — Immediate Cancellation

The Situation

Core partner at $105.33/month with 3 Fair Harbor listings. Cited "no ROI" and "too expensive." Zero bookings in 435 days. Profile incomplete — no logo, missing reviews and videos. Also had a prior cancellation request in July 2025.

Decision: Process Immediate Cancellation (May 18)

The prior cancellation request was the key signal — this partner already mentally checked out almost a year ago and was pushed to stay. A hard-save attempt would come across as predatory. Respect the decision.

Lesson

Prior cancellation attempts signal a firm decision. Don't fight it twice — it damages the relationship and wastes call time. Send cancellation confirmation email with Batch Partner Policy reference.

Saved (6-Month Window)
Camp Tiki — Churn Delay & Optimization

The Situation

Partner at $150/month on a 12-month commitment. Requested cancellation immediately after a $825 semi-annual payment (with $75 discount already applied). Cited "too expensive" and "not seeing enough ROI." Still within the commitment term.

Decision: Enforce Commitment, Schedule for October 31

Enforced the 12-month term. To justify the remaining 6 months, immediately offered a full optimization package: copy 80 reviews from one listing to another, reshuffle media, activate UGC Collection Tool, create a blog post, re-optimize listings with new keywords.

Outcome

Partner accepted the plan. 6-month window created to demonstrate value. Schedule 30/60/90-day follow-ups to track progress and build the case for renewal.

Lesson

Enforce the commitment once — and immediately back it up with action. The commitment gives you time; the optimization package gives you a reason to use it.

Churned (Commitment Waived)
Borgz — Immediate Cancellation

The Situation

Partner at $99/month on a 12-month commitment. Claimed a misunderstanding about the monthly payment structure. Profile incomplete — no videos, no reviews. Zero impressions — no SEO traction at all.

Decision: Immediate Cancellation (May 29) — Commitment Waived

Zero impressions means nothing to point to. The "misled" claim, even if not fully accurate, made enforcing the policy too risky. A partner who feels deceived will file a dispute. Processing the cancellation immediately cost less than a potential dispute loss.

Lesson

When a partner claims they were misled and there's genuinely no performance data to show them, enforcing the commitment makes Batch look predatory. Cut the loss cleanly.
